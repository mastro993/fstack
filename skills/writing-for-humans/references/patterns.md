# Patterns to catch

Sentence habits outlast word fashions: each model generation swaps its favorite words, but the shapes below stay. Scan for patterns first and words last. Each line reads: pattern, example, rewrite.

## Sentences

- Negative parallelism: "It's not a tool, it's a mindset." / "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." → say what it is.
- Rhetorical question answered at once: "The result? Faster builds." → "Builds are faster."
- Significance tail: "…, underscoring its pivotal role in the region." → end at the fact.
- Short tail after a comma: "…, and that matters." → cut it.
- Padded triple: "fast, secure, and seamless" with one fact behind it → name that fact.
- Inflated verbs: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" → "is", "has".
- "From X to Y" with no real range between them → list the actual items.
- The same opening on sentence after sentence → vary or merge them.

## Structure

- Preamble that announces what follows: "Here's what you need to know:" → start with it.
- Teacher voice and false suspense: "Let's break this down.", "Here's the kicker:" → state the point.
- Narrating your own process: "First, I looked at the logs…" → give the finding.
- Counting before listing: "There are three reasons." / "The first… The second…" in prose → give the reasons.
- Evidence piled up before the point → point first, evidence after.
- Defending a point nobody disputes → cut the defense.
- Summaries at every level, and "In conclusion" → one summary at most, at the top.
- Closing by tying back to the question, or with a line that sounds wise and says nothing → stop at the last fact.
- Reusing an earlier phrase as a payoff → cut the callback.
- One point restated several ways → keep the clearest one.
- Short punchy fragments as their own paragraphs → join them into sentences.
- A "challenges" paragraph ending in "the future looks bright" → the problem and its status.

## Tone

- Vague attribution: "Experts argue", "Studies show", "a classic", "famously" → name the source or drop it.
- Inflated stakes: "This changes everything." → what changes, for whom.
- Coined labels: "the X paradox", "the X trap" → describe the thing.
- Forced metaphors, "Think of it as…", "Imagine a world where…" → the literal claim.
- Sales language: vibrant, nestled, groundbreaking, renowned, game-changing → a concrete fact.
- Switching between "I" and "we" → pick one.
- Chat leftovers: "Certainly!", "Great question!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if…", "as of my last update" → delete.

## Formatting

- Title Case Headings → sentence case.
- Headings built on Why / What / Where ("Why it matters") → name the content.
- A bold label at the start of every bullet → plain bullets.
- More than one em dash per paragraph → commas, colons, parentheses.
- Arrows and other hard-to-type characters in prose → words.
- Emoji in headings or bullets → remove.
- A table holding two or three values → a sentence.
- Raw `##`, `**`, or citation tokens where markdown doesn't render → plain text.

## Words

Flag these when several appear together; one alone is fine. Replace with the plain word or, better, the fact.

- Overused by models: additionally (also), align with (match), bolster (support), crucial / pivotal / key (important, or cut), delve (look at), emphasize / underscore / highlight (show, or cut), enduring, enhance (improve), foster (help), garner (get), interplay, intricate, landscape (field, market), meticulous (careful), showcase (show), tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant.
- Also flagged: commendable, compelling, ecosystem, harness (use), leverage (use), navigate (handle), noteworthy, nuance, paradigm, realm, resonate, robust (strong), seamless, streamline (simplify), synergy, versatile.
- Magic adverbs: quietly, deeply, fundamentally, remarkably, truly.
- Filler transitions: "It's worth noting", notably, importantly, interestingly.
- Office jargon: agenda (outside meetings), deliver (for anything but parcels), drive (for anything but vehicles), empower, facilitate (help), impact as a verb (affect), liaise (work with), overarching, tackle (fix, handle), transform, utilize (use).
